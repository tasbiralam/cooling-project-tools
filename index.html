🎯 Project Objective

  3. Verify — Ran full CFD (COMSOL Multiphysics) on the actual CAD geometry to check the prediction.

Goal: show that hand-calculation and CFD agree, demonstrating both design and analytical engineering skills.

CFD Result Visuals & Interpretation

Highest at the blade tip

Velocity scales directly with radius (v = ω × r), so the tip moves fastest and shows the highest velocity, while the hub and far edge of the domain stay close to zero.

Differs across each blade face

The pressure side pushes air (higher pressure) while the suction side pulls air along (lower pressure) — this difference generates thrust, the same principle as lift on a wing.

Note: the Inlet and Outlet boundaries were set to a fixed Pressure = 0 Pa condition (a modeling choice, not a measured result), so domain-average pressure isn’t meaningful here. The local pressure difference across each blade, shown above, is what reflects the fan’s actual aerodynamic loading.

🔍 The Science Behind It

Why velocity is highest at the blade tip:
Since v = ω × r, velocity scales directly with radius. The blade tip (largest r) moves fastest, so it shows the highest velocity in the contour plot. Near the hub (r ≈ 0) and outside the domain, velocity drops close to zero.

Why pressure differs across each blade face:
Each blade has a pressure side (the face pushing the air — higher pressure) and a suction side (the trailing face pulling air along — lower pressure). This pressure difference across the blade is what generates thrust/airflow, the same principle as lift on an airplane wing.
